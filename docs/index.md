# National Park Camping Reservations: How to Book a Site

Camping inside a US national park — waking up to the sound of elk in Rocky Mountain, or watching the sun rise over the Grand Canyon from your tent — is one of the best outdoor experiences in the country. But the most popular park campgrounds book out fast. Here is how the reservation system works and how to give yourself the best chance of getting a site.

Recreation.gov is where most bookings happen. Most reservable campgrounds in national parks are booked through Recreation.gov, the federal booking system. You will need a free account. For many parks, sites are released six months in advance, on a rolling basis — so a site for 15 July becomes available on 15 January. Popular parks and holiday weekends can sell out within minutes of release, so know your dates before the window opens and be ready at the release time.

Have your details ready. Before booking, know your arrival and departure dates, the size of your party, your vehicle and tent size, and whether you need an accessible site. Some campgrounds ask for vehicle length because sites have limits. A booking fee applies per reservation. Read the cancellation and change policy before you pay — rules vary by park.

First-come, first-served loops still exist. Not every park campground takes reservations. Many parks keep some loops first-come, first-served, especially in the shoulder seasons. For these, arrive early in the day — ideally before noon — and have a backup plan. Rangers at the visitor center can usually tell you which campgrounds still have space. Weekdays are much easier than weekends.

If the park is full, look just outside it. When a park campground is booked out, look at the national forests and state parks around it. Forest Service campgrounds are often reservable on the same Recreation.gov system, and they put you within a short drive of the park entrance. Private campgrounds near park gateways are another option. Dispersed camping on nearby national forest land is free in many areas, but check local rules first — it is not allowed everywhere, and fire restrictions apply.

Check alerts before you travel. Before you leave, check the park's official alerts page for road closures, fire restrictions, bear activity, and campground status. Keep your reservation confirmation — on paper or saved offline on your phone — because cell signal inside parks is unreliable.
