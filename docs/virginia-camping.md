# Camping in Virginia: Blue Ridge, Shenandoah and the Coast

Virginia packs a remarkable range of camping into one mid-Atlantic state: the Blue Ridge Mountains and Shenandoah National Park in the west, rolling Piedmont in the middle, and the Chesapeake Bay and Atlantic coast in the east. It is an easy state for a first camping trip and a rewarding one for experienced hikers, with fall color that rivals anywhere in the country.

## Shenandoah National Park

Shenandoah is Virginia's camping centerpiece, a long, narrow park running along the Blue Ridge with the Skyline Drive as its spine. The park's campgrounds — Big Meadows, Lewis Mountain, Loft Mountain, and Mathews Arm — sit at elevation, so nights run cool even in summer. Fall is the great season here, when the ridges turn red and gold, but it is also the busiest, so reserve early. Black bears are common in the park; use the food-storage lockers and never leave food in your tent. The park's waterfalls and viewpoints are best reached on weekday mornings, before the overlooks fill.

## The Blue Ridge and the Appalachian Trail

South of Shenandoah, the Blue Ridge Parkway continues into the high country, and the Appalachian Trail crosses some of its finest ridgelines. Campgrounds in the national forests — places like Sherando Lake and the Crabtree Falls area — offer quieter alternatives to the park campgrounds, with lake swimming and waterfall hikes close by. This is classic eastern mountain camping: hardwood forest, cool streams, and mornings that start in mist. Spring brings wildflowers, summer brings humidity and afternoon thunderstorms, and winter brings real cold at elevation, so check the forecast and pack layers.

## The coast and the Chesapeake

East, Virginia's camping changes completely. First Landing State Park near Virginia Beach offers wooded campsites minutes from the ocean, and the Eastern Shore's barrier islands — like Assateague, shared with Maryland — are famous for wild ponies and long, empty beaches. Coastal camping means sand in everything, steady wind, and mosquitoes in summer, so bring a shade shelter and repellent. Fall is the sweet spot on the coast: warm water, fewer crowds, and clear skies.

## Planning your Virginia trip

Whatever part of Virginia you choose, a little planning goes a long way. Start your planning at the [CampAndTrailGuide homepage](https://campandtrailguide.com/), check your gear against the [beginners camping checklist](https://campandtrailguide.com/blog/beginners-camping-checklist/), and review the [gear guide](https://campandtrailguide.com/gear.html) before you pack. Reserve early for fall weekends in Shenandoah, and check the forecast for your exact area.

Virginia rewards campers who take their time. Hike a ridge in the morning, swim a lake in the afternoon, and you will find a state that offers a different trip in every direction.
